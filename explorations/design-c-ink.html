Ink & Lantern

Chinese ink painting meets the modern explainer. Every short is a sheet of rice paper: ideas arrive as brush strokes, verdicts land as carved seals, and Lóng the river dragon guides the viewer from a vague prompt to a clear one.

Character

Meet Lóng

Four poses cover every beat of a short. Lóng always faces left, toward the content, so the viewer's eye follows the dragon's gaze to the prompt.

Lóng 龙

A young river dragon on day one of the job: brilliant, eager, and missing all your context.

Give Lóng a clear brief and it moves mountains. Give it a vague one and it guesses, then looks at you, puzzled.

Pose 1

Listening

Default companion. On screen while a prompt types on.

Pose 2

Puzzled

After a vague prompt. Scratches its mane, question mark bobs.

Pose 3

Delighted

When the good result lands. Hops, gold sparks twinkle.

Pose 4

Explaining

Tip cards and the why. Points with a calligraphy brush.

  • 一Red body, gold horns and belly plates, ink mane. Never recolor Lóng.
  • 二Idle motion is always on: float 4 s, blink every 5 s, whiskers sway.
  • 三Pose swaps are instant cuts, never crossfades. The cut is the joke.
  • 四Lóng sits in the lower third, left side. Content owns the upper two thirds.
Color

Paper, ink, and one seal

Prompt card · clear

Your response will be read aloud by a text-to-speech engine, so never use ellipses since the text-to-speech engine will not know how to pronounce them.

The reason lets Claude generalize: it also avoids other things a voice can't read.
